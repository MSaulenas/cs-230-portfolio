# cs-230-portfolio

- Briefly summarize The Gaming Room client and their software requirements. Who was the client? What type of software did they want you to design?
  - The Gaming Room is a videogame development studio who was looking to expand the supported client devices of their game, "Draw It or Lose It". They wanted to achieve this by creating an application that runs in the web browser as well as an app on mobile devices.
- What did you do particularly well in developing this documentation?
  - I believe I did particularly well when it came to defining the software development life cycle for the application under the "Development Tools" section. This is the area I am most passionate in. I have wanted be a DevOps or Platform Engineer for nearly a decade now.
- What about the process of working through a design document did you find helpful when developing the code?
  - I actually have prior experience reviewing and creating these documents in a profession setting. Although, I think it helped me reinforce my confidence when it comes to identifying outdated approaches.
- If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?
  - I would probably revise the section for Project One. I do not think I properly realized the expectations at that time. I thought the entire design document was going to be that project and that project 2-3 were going to be implementing it. This led to me not being focused enough on those sections.
- How did you interpret the user’s needs and implement them into your software design? Why is it so important to consider the user’s needs when designing?
  - Without the users, there is no need for your product to be created. You must consider the user experience from beginning to end. A poor experience will turn away new users and stall your business growth.
- How did you approach designing software? What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a similar software application?
  - I focused heavily on a microservice architecture. I think it allows for incredibly simple software lifecycle management. I believe that an improperly managed software development lifecycle is the number one way to impede progress and stall a project.
